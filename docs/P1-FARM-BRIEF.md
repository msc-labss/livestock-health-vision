# What we would ask of a dairy, and what it costs you

A one-page brief for a farm considering hosting the study. Written to be handed
over as it is. Everything here is what we would commit to in writing before
anything is installed.

---

## In one paragraph

We are a university research project measuring whether a single camera can
detect lameness in dairy cows early. We would mount one camera above a lane your
cows already walk through, record silently for a few months, and have trained
scorers assess mobility on the same animals. **The system tells you nothing
during the study and you change nothing about how you farm.** In exchange your
herd gets regularly mobility-scored, by trained scorers, at no cost to you, and
you keep those records.

---

## What we need from you

| | |
|---|---|
| **A lane** | One your cows already pass through single file — parlour exit, AMS exit, or a race. Roughly 80–160 cows passing it, twice daily is ideal. |
| **A camera mount** | Somewhere to fix one camera looking along the lane from the side, with mains power and either network or somewhere to leave a recorder. |
| **Occasional access** | Installation takes a morning. After that, a visit every few weeks to check it. |
| **Your cow IDs** | Whatever your parlour or AMS already records — which cow passed, and when. This is the single thing that makes the study possible; without it the footage cannot be matched to animals. |
| **Your own lameness figures** | Roughly what proportion of the herd you currently see as lame, from your own records. It tells us whether the study is viable on your herd before we start. |
| **A conversation about staff** | The camera will sometimes catch people. We mask them automatically, but you and we need to agree signage and who can see footage. |

## What it costs you

Honestly: **a morning for installation, and agreeing a scoring day each week.**

Nothing else. We do not handle your animals, we do not ask you to change
treatment, trimming, routines or anything else, and we do not need you to do
anything with the system.

## What you get

- **Your herd mobility-scored weekly by trained scorers, free**, for the length
  of the study. Most farms pay for this.
- **All of those records**, in whatever form is useful to you.
- No obligation to act on any of it, and no obligation to continue.

---

## What we will not do

- **We will not tell you a cow is lame during the study.** The system runs
  silently. This is deliberate: if it told you something and it were wrong, it
  would have changed how you farm on the strength of a research prototype.
- **We will not ask you to delay treating an animal.** Anything a scorer or your
  staff spots is yours to treat immediately, as you would anyway. The study
  measures against the record made *before* treatment; it never asks for
  treatment to be withheld to lengthen a measurement.
- **We will not publish anything identifying your farm** without your written
  agreement.
- **We will not keep footage of identifiable people.** Faces and bodies are
  masked automatically, and any clip that cannot be masked is not retained.

## What we cannot promise

The system does not currently produce any clinical result, and it may never work
well enough to. This is research, and the honest position is that we are
measuring whether it works at all. Nothing it produces during the study is
veterinary advice, and none of it should be used to make a decision about an
animal.

---

## Roughly how it runs

```
  week 0      one morning: mount the camera, check the recording is usable
  week 1      first scoring visit; recording begins in earnest
  weeks 1-n   silent recording; scorers visit weekly
  end         you keep the scoring records; camera comes down
```

Long enough to see cases develop — some months rather than some weeks. We would
agree the end date with you before starting, and you can stop at any point.

## The one thing that might surprise you

We need **two** trained scorers looking at the same cows independently, not one.

That is because scorers disagree with each other more than people expect. In a
published study, ten experienced scorers looking at the same 207 cows estimated
lameness anywhere between 36% and 57% of the herd. If we compared our system
against a single scorer we would mostly be measuring that disagreement. Two
scorers, plus a third opinion where they differ, gives us something stable to
measure against.

We supply and pay for the scorers. It costs you the time to have them on the
farm.

Questions about anything above, including the parts that sound inconvenient, are
welcome before any commitment. Nothing is installed until the terms above are
agreed in writing.
